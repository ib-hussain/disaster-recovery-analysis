1. ISO
3-letter country code
2. year
Year of the governance estimate 
3. indicator
This specifies which of the six WGI governance indicators the row refers to.
Codes (from WGI documentation):
Code	Meaning
va	Voice and Accountability
pv	Political Stability / Absence of Violence
ge	Government Effectiveness
rq	Regulatory Quality
rl	Rule of Law
cc	Control of Corruption
4. estimate → Govindex
This is the core governance score, what you are calling Govindex.
Definition:
A standardized score ranging roughly from –2.5 (very weak) to +2.5 (very strong) governance performance.
It is a z-score (standard normal unit).
Interpretation:
Value	Meaning
+2.0	exceptionally strong governance
+1.0	above average
0.0	global average
–1.0	weak governance
–2.0	extremely weak governance
Example:
AFG, 1996, cc, -1.29 → Afghanistan had very poor control of corruption.
5. stddev
Standard error of the estimate
Shows the uncertainty around the governance score
Higher = less certain, lower = more precise
6. nsource
Number of independent data sources used to estimate governance for that country & year.
WGI combines multiple surveys, expert assessments, business indicators.
Example:
nsource = 6 (Australia) = strong, consistent measurement
nsource = 1 (Antigua) = sparse data, less reliable
7. pctrank
This is the percentile rank (0–100):
“How does the country compare to all others in the world?”
Example:
Australia 1996: pctrank = 93.5
→ Australia is better than 93.5% of all countries in Control of Corruption.
8. pctranklower
Lower bound of the percentile rank’s 90% confidence interval.
Example:
Argentina 1996:
pctrank = 53.8
pctranklower = 38.7
→ We are 90% confident Argentina is at least at the 38.7th percentile.
9. pctrankupper
Upper bound of the percentile rank’s 90% confidence interval.
Example:
Argentina:
pctrankupper = 62.9
Meaning:
Argentina’s governance performance lies between the 38.7th and 62.9th percentile with 90% confidence.
